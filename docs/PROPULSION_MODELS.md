# EV and hydrogen models — integrated in 0.0.237

## 0.0.264 charging curves in stop selection

Before 0.0.264 the stop optimizer charged every kWh at one average kW per charger (`kw = 0.7 × min(car peak, station)`), so in Fastest mode a top-up from 70→90% looked as quick as 10→30%; the taper appeared only afterwards, in the thermal validation. Now:

- **The curve.** `chargecurve.js` `ChargeCurve.forCar(car)` = the curve typed in Garage → Edit → Energy & charging estimates (`car.dcCurve`, kW at 0, 10 … 100%), else a bundled Open EV Data curve for the matched make / model words / variant / drive / usable battery / year (scaled to the car's typed or Brave peak when there is one), else a generic shape scaled to the peak (150 kW when none). The generic shape is steeper than 0.0.263's (72% of peak at 50%, 43% at 70%, 30% at 80%, 17% at 90%; previously 83 / 63 / 50 / 34%), between the owner's Model Y figures and the dataset's curves.
- **Stop selection.** `trip.js` `chargeTimes` builds, per charger, cumulative minutes from empty to every planning level: power = min(curve at that SOC, charger kW × sharing × 0.98), 0.93 stored/terminal (AC: min(car AC, station), tapering above 90%, 0.9). A stop from level *f* to *g* costs `T[g] − T[f]`, which keeps the DP's running-minimum transition exact (tests/charge-curve.test.js checks it against brute force). Fastest mode minimizes these minutes plus detour and stop setup, so it charges low in the battery and often when chargers are close together, and charges higher only to cross long gaps. Cheapest overall still minimizes cost; curve minutes only break exact cost ties (1e-6 $/min).
- **Validation.** `Propulsion` uses the same curve (capped at the peak) in its thermal charging simulation, so the reported minutes match what was optimized, before the temperature effects the model already has.

Limits: Open EV Data curves are often coarse (4 points) and some entries look wrong (e.g. a 57.5 kW Model 3); the editor shows the source so the user can correct it. US coverage is incomplete (no Rivian, no Kia EV6 here). Temperature and charger-specific behavior are still estimates. This is the exact optimum for the planner's model, not a guarantee for real charging sessions.

## 0.0.245 coverage and persistence corrections

EV discovery now uses a fixed corridor covering the supported 30-minute extra-driving limit (the existing geometric road-distance approximation, not a guarantee of road access). Planning still enforces the selected 1.0–30.0 minutes. Narrower old saves need one broader discovery; subsequent limit changes reuse inventory/tariffs. Tesla/Mirai demos default to 30 minutes; explicit edits remain editable. No green band is a valid result when no tested increase meets the percentage balance rule; the tile now explains it. Failed lite previews are checked again with full-planner options before being declared infeasible.

The owner's backup contained an empty saved-trip file and a valid history index; the precise interruption/error causing that empty write is unknown. All native KV writes now replace a completed temporary file instead of truncating the final file first, with trip-file synchronization, checked save results and size/time/error diagnostics. Only completed saved-record writes may advance history. Persisted inventory omits stations outside every possible route-leg corridor, without pruning live mixed-route discovery data needed for recovery. This protects future writes; it cannot recover geometry or tariffs absent from an already empty file.

## 0.0.244 consumption graph and saved prices

The EV/H2 graph now follows the fossil-fuel graph: driving kWh/100 mi (EV) or kg/100 mi (hydrogen) versus requested speed offset. Straight diagonal segments connect computed scenarios; infeasible gaps are not bridged. Separate buttons select the lowest-energy and lowest-energy-cost tested plans (ties prefer shorter total trip time). These are minima among tested settings, not global optima. Green indicates that the percentage energy increase between adjacent settings is no greater than the percentage driving-time decrease, analogous to the gas graph. It is a balance guide, not a promise of lowest cost, fastest total trip, safety or exact optimum. Charging/stop choices and different tariffs can separate minimum energy from minimum cost. The selected summary shows actual modeled trip energy cost and total-time differences versus posted limits (or the first feasible sample). Per-road controls, speed rules/caps, weather, and discrete feasible-only application remain.

Saved trips retain EV/H2 raw station inventories and original tariff timestamps, including previously searched alternatives. Cold reopening reprojects these prices without website reads; it does not restore alternative-route consent. New coverage metadata scopes the inventory by connector/charging-level/provider settings, Tesla-owner pricing eligibility, exact route geometry and searched corridor. Wider corridors or changed discovery settings require discovery; explicit refresh bypasses reuse. Legacy nonempty matching route inventories can be replayed but lack verified coverage metadata. Incomplete scans are not newly marked complete. Saving a narrower selection preserves previous inventories; tariffs stay within scoped inventories rather than a cross-vehicle global union. Price-age preferences still apply without advancing timestamps.

## Previous 0.0.240–243 speed graph (superseded)

0.0.243 clarifies the same finite-difference calculation: connected steps, a labeled zero baseline with no invented negative range, no fake starting measurement, and regular five-mph axis ticks even when the current setting is +9. The caption includes the full trip mileage and speed cap. Large minute changes can be real over a multi-thousand-mile trip (2,632 miles at 45 versus 50 mph differs by about 351 driving minutes). Debug logs now retain each sample’s driving, active charging, elapsed time, cost, average speed and normalized intervals; a screenshot alone cannot validate the charging series.

The EV/H2 tile compares **driving minutes saved** with **extra active charging/refueling minutes**, on one shared scale normalized to each +5 mph increase. Values are finite differences between adjacent feasible, explicitly tested scenarios; steps do not interpolate across failed plans or claim a smooth/global optimum. Each point also reports whole-trip energy cost and its difference from the selected road's at-limit scenario (other roads unchanged). The first feasible sample is the cost reference if at-limit is infeasible. Requested offsets are relative to posted limits, not average trip speed. Caps may flatten the curves.

Green means a tested increase reduces total simulated elapsed trip time, including detours, stop setup, destination stays and scheduled waits. It does **not** mean cheapest, legal or safest. Charging can overlap a planned stay, and stop changes can cause jumps: merely comparing the two lines is not sufficient to establish an optimum. The projection exposes active charging time separately from total non-driving time for this purpose. Hydrogen remains explicitly unvalidated.

Choose All roads or one specific road occurrence (identified by its name and route-mile bounds). Per-road overrides retain other roads' defaults/choices and are scoped to the route geometry/instructions, persisted with the trip and cleared for new trips or explicit default-rule edits. Known sections honor truck limits, the over-limit opt-in and the speed cap. Unknown sections retain route speeds. Forecast/geometry/settings changes invalidate samples; switching between unchanged road graphs reuses them. Trials share one departure/forecast snapshot and use saved stations only, in cancellable background jobs. A forecast refresh retains the previous hourly temperature series while loading, without retaining completed hazard approval; out-of-range temperatures remain flagged.

## Previous 0.0.238 speed graph (superseded)

The tile now graphs energy-cost change (left dollar axis) and driving-plus-stop-time change (right minute axis) against modeled average driving speed. Independent axes mean an intersection has **no optimization meaning**. Green marks sampled at/below-limit choices that cost less without adding total time versus the at-limit scenario; it is not a fitted optimum. Infeasible samples remain gaps and cannot be applied. The slider and keyboard/tap targets select discrete evaluated scenarios, not interpolated feasibility.

Default offset and the “no faster than” cap follow the trip's normal speed rule, including leaving roads already at/above that cap at their limit. Above-limit scenarios appear only when the user has enabled the over-limit rule; they are not automatically endorsed or shaded green. The time adjustment adds the same per-mile `1/(limit + offset) - 1/limit` term used by the fossil-fuel controls to the route's baseline time. This is an estimate, not a traffic simulation. Changed timing feeds back into weather, energy use and charging. Graph work uses cached stations/forecast series in background workers, with no price/discovery requests. Signs, minimum speeds, traffic and conditions override any app setting.

## What changed

`Propulsion` is a pure, offline module serialized into planner workers. It reuses selected-route hourly temperatures retained by `RouteWx`, including weather changes during charging. Missing/out-of-range temperatures use **20°C with a visible warning**, never a newly invented forecast. Speed trials reuse existing candidates/weather and make no discovery calls. They compare at/below-limit offsets, plus user-enabled over-limit scenarios, against known posted limits; unknown-limit stretches keep the route's original speed. Google average segment speeds are not a traffic simulator. These are estimated tradeoffs, **not a proven optimal speed or globally optimal stop plan**.

The existing cost optimizer proposes stops. Up to three energy/stop-clock passes update consumption and simulate actual charging, then check arrival energy, reserves and numerical agreement. Failure is explicit, not a feasible result. The original, unadjusted route is retained so repeated replanning does not compound weather adjustments. Cost optimization still uses posted per-energy prices, not a joint nonlinear charging/stop/speed search; taper and auxiliary consumption can change the best real-world plan.

Alternative-route checks and acceptance compose the selected legs' cached speed-limit sections and hourly temperatures on the joined trip's mileage scale. EV/H2 routes without turn instructions use the existing geometry-based HPMS lookup (unknown stretches retain route speeds and uncertainty flags). Primary planning waits for this context instead of first promising a route at incomplete speed settings. Accepting an EV recovery route retains its validated plan during hazard review; an explicit manual return to Hazards still starts a new review when remembering choices is off. A later infeasible energy recalculation replaces the successful result rather than retaining obsolete stops. Cached station reprojection does not request another network price pass, and off-corridor sites are filtered before website pricing. The graph design is unchanged in 0.0.239 pending the owner's rate-based/per-road design review.

Charging respects vehicle power, station power, explicit sharing fraction if available, SOC target/cap, conversion losses, cabin/12 V loads, and estimated pack cooling. Cooling is inside the inlet budget. DC session limit is four hours; public AC is 24 hours, destination AC up to seven days. Billed energy is separate from stored energy, so purchased/consumed-energy costs reconcile in Stops, Depart and vehicle comparison without altering the posted station tariff. Unknown DC billing basis assumes DC metering and is flagged; only explicitly AC-metered DC stations use the EPFL conversion assumption. Destination duration mode supports partial charge and parking after reaching a cap. Destination previews remain generic, and the final thermal projection can reject an optimistic preview.

## Profiles and evidence

1. **Default EV estimate.** Retains EPA/observed baseline consumption; uses a generic road-load *relative* speed shape, additional cold/hot HVAC load, capacity-scaled thermal response and a normalized generic DC taper. This is not a measured curve for that car. Do not use gasoline ORNL speed-loss percentages. Temperature-specific fast-charge derating is unknown for these cars, not inherited from LFP.
2. **Explicitly confirmed TUM profile.** Garage → Edit vehicle → Energy & charging estimates. The user must confirm the **2020 Model 3 SR+ CATL LFP (~55.4 kWh)**. No model-name or chemistry guessing. Derived from 44 dyno runs, 67 warmup drives and a warm/cold DC session pair for one vehicle. Drive polynomial uses m/s and watts. Pack thermal mass 479 kJ/K, fitted driving UA and heat-generation terms; parked UA 22.2 W/K. DC stored/terminal ratio .933. These are one-car fitted estimates, not fleet validation. Below 19%/above 83% SOC and cold charging below 19°C are extrapolated and flagged. Low-speed consumption falls back to the EPA/observed baseline. Optional heat pump and 3 kW preconditioning are assumptions, not verified vehicle controls. Starting pack temperature defaults to ambient unless entered. Return-time/destination dwell cools the pack; brief stops do not reset it to ambient.
3. **Hydrogen estimate.** Baseline kg/mile is preserved. A relative road-load/fuel-cell curve compares slower speeds, but is **unvalidated** and marked as such. kg/hour is divided by speed to obtain kg/distance; there is no universal cold/hot multiplier. Retail fueling retains 1 kg/min plus three setup minutes, not a claimed SAE J2601 pressure/pre-cooling simulation.

The 45°C cooling threshold, COP 2, generic thermal mass/6-hour soak, generic HVAC curves and efficiency factors are assumptions. The measured warm charging session itself reaches hotter pack temperatures. Negative power is bounded; the app conservatively does not credit net downhill regeneration to range because the existing reserve DP assumes monotone energy burn. It also lacks verified grade/traffic, wind, degradation, queue, occupancy and charger-operational data. Optional final top-up temperature uses the plan's ending pack-temperature estimate and is flagged as approximate. No queue or outage is inferred from a network's maximum kW.
